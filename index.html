D  ·  Worth leaving for the specialist  ·  1 items

Needs measuring before fixing, and the fix carries real regression risk.

Ticks and names are stored in the shared worklist and are visible to everyone with this page. The date is stamped for you.

The headline

Three things would actively mislead an incoming specialist. Everything else is ordinary tidying.

The two storefronts are wired together as a UK/US pair, but they don't share product URLs — the UK sells /products/sparkling-rose where the US sells /products/rose. The international linking was built by mirroring the URL path across domains, so on the brand's three flagship wines it points at addresses that redirect or don't exist. Separately, both sites tell search engines they are the same company entity, under a single identifier that carries three different company names. And the homepage of each site has no H1 at all.

Blocking — fix before any SEO work begins

These distort the measurements a specialist would take in week one, so doing them later means redoing the analysis.

SEO-01Theme · gatedBlocking 32 of 131 pages · both sites

International (hreflang) links point at redirects and dead URLs, including all three flagship wines

Each page declares a UK and a US counterpart by taking its own path and swapping the domain. That assumption holds for 99 pages and fails for 32, because the two stores use different handles for the same wine. Google discards a whole hreflang cluster when the return link doesn't resolve, so for the affected pages the two sites currently read as unrelated duplicates competing with each other rather than as regional variants.

What needs doing

Replace path-mirroring with an explicit UK↔US map keyed on the product, and emit an alternate only where a real counterpart exists. Where a page genuinely has no counterpart, omit the tag rather than guessing. The x-default needs the same treatment — it currently points at UK 404s.

SEO-02Decision + AdminBlocking All 131 pages

One company identifier carries three different company names

Both sites publish their Organization data under the single identifier bolledrinks.co.uk/#organization. That is a legitimate choice — one global brand entity — but the same identifier is used for three different names on the same page, which makes the entity self-contradictory at exactly the moment answer engines are trying to resolve who BOLLE is.

On the US storefront that entity also declares "url": "https://www.bolledrinks.co.uk" and a London, GB address as its only location — so the US site publishes no US-facing entity signal at all. This compounds a problem already logged internally: the Shopify vendor field is inconsistent across products (some BOLLE, some Bolle Drinks), and that field feeds the same markup plus every sales-channel feed.

What needs doing

Decide once whether BOLLE is one global entity or two regional ones, then make names consistent everywhere: use name for the trading name and legalName for the registered company, never a third variant on the same identifier. Align the vendor field in Shopify in the same pass — it is the upstream source.

SEO-03ThemeBlocking Both homepages · 131 pages for hierarchy

Neither homepage has an H1, and every page's headings begin at H3

Both homepages carry roughly 840 words with no H1 element. Separately, the mobile navigation drawer renders three <h3> section titles — Shop, Learn, Contact — before the main content on every page, so the document outline opens at H3 and then jumps back to H1. Product pages then skip H1 → H3 again for the size options.

Heading order in source, US product page
  • h3 "Shop" ← nav drawer
  • h3 "Learn" ← nav drawer
  • h3 "Contact" ← nav drawer
  • h1 "Blanc de Blancs" ← the actual page title
  • h3 "750ml Bottle" / "375ml Demi" / "1.5L Magnum"
  • h2 "Awards"
What needs doing

The drawer titles are navigation labels, not document structure — change them to div or span with the existing styling. That single edit corrects the outline on all 131 pages. Then give each homepage a real H1 carrying its primary term, and demote the product size-option headings to H2-level or below the fold structure.

SEO-04Theme + AdminBlocking 80 of 126 pages

Every title has the shop name appended twice over, and blog titles are cut mid-word

The theme appends – Bolle Drinks USA / – BOLLE Drinks UK unless the title already contains the shop name. Titles say "BOLLE", not "Bolle Drinks USA", so the guard never fires and the suffix is always added — pushing 80 titles past the point where Google truncates them. On blog posts the underlying title is separately capped around 70 characters, so the suffix lands after a word that has already been chopped.

SEO-06ThemeHigh 23 of 32 products

Most products publish no review stars, and FAQ markup is limited to a hard-coded list of five

The product markup itself is well built — it carries price validity, shipping, returns, condition and barcode. The gap is coverage. Review ratings appear on only a handful of products, so the rest forfeit star ratings in search results. FAQ content is gated on a hand-written list of product handles, meaning every product added since is silently excluded.

Drive review markup from the review source for every product rather than per-product conditions, and replace the handle whitelist with FAQ content held on the product itself. Add breadcrumbs across collections and blog posts — they are the cheapest of these and affect the most pages.

SEO-07ThemeHigh Product template

Unrecognised review scores are silently published as 4 stars

Review ratings are converted from words to numbers with a fallback that assigns 4 out of 5 to anything unmatched, so a review whose wording changes — or an empty value — is published to Google as a positive rating that nobody gave. Related: the mpn field is filled with the product's URL handle rather than a manufacturer part number.

sections/main-product-bolle.liquid
  • {% case review.overall %}
  • {% when 'Exceptional' %}5 {% when 'Excellent' %}5
  • {% when 'Good' %}4 {% when 'Poor' %}2
  • {% else %}4 ← anything unrecognised becomes 4 stars
What needs doing

Make the fallback omit the review rather than invent a score. Ratings that can't be traced to a real review are a policy risk on top of an accuracy one. Either drop mpn or populate it properly — gtin13 is already correct.

AEO-01Theme · your copyHigh Both sites

The agent-facing files are Shopify's boilerplate and say nothing about the wine

Both sites serve /llms.txt and /agents.md, and are listed in an agent-discovery sitemap — good foundations, all present and correct. But the content is Shopify's generated checkout guidance: how a shopping agent should transact. There is nothing about what BOLLE is, how it is made, what it has won, or how it differs from the category. An answer engine reading these files learns how to buy but not why to recommend.

The raw material for this already exists and is strong: the FAQ page carries 28 questions in machine-readable form matching what visitors see, and the product pages carry detailed twice-fermentation and awards content.

What needs doing

Author a real llms.txt — what the product is, the twice-fermentation process, the award record with sources, where it is stocked, and links to the canonical pages for each. Also decide explicitly whether AI crawlers are welcome: robots.txt currently names none of them, so the answer is "yes" by default rather than by decision.

SEO-09ThemeMedium Theme files

Two competing product-markup implementations, and the weaker one is still wired up

The good markup lives in main-product-bolle.liquid. An older structured-data-product.liquid — no ratings, no barcode, no shipping or returns, and hard-coded to http://schema.org — is still rendered by three other sections. None of them is on a live product page today, so nothing is currently broken; it is a trap waiting for whoever next changes a template.

What needs doing

Delete the legacy snippet and repoint the three sections, or leave a comment on it saying which one is authoritative.

SEO-10ThemeMedium Theme files

The theme hard-codes itself to the UK, which will silently break a US redeploy

layout/theme.liquid opens with assign force_current_site = 'uk', and snippets/head-tag.liquid writes a canonical tag pointing at bolledrinks.co.uk on every page regardless of which store renders it. The live US site serves correct self-referencing canonicals today, so this is not currently causing harm — but copying this theme to the US store would canonicalise the entire US catalogue to the UK and remove it from US results.

What needs doing

Derive the domain from the request rather than a literal, so the theme is safe to deploy to either store. Worth doing before any theme work begins, since an SEO engagement usually starts with template edits.

SEO-11AdminMedium 131 pages

Language is declared as generic English while the alternates claim regional variants

Every page on both sites declares <html lang="en">, while the hreflang tags on the same page claim en-gb and en-us. The two should agree. Low impact on its own; trivial to fix alongside SEO-01.

Not measured

Named so the gaps are explicit rather than assumed covered.

Core Web Vitals were not measured. Google's testing API refused the requests on quota, so no page-speed figure here is real. What can be said from the HTML alone: pages weigh 200–280KB before images, with 43–48 separate inline script blocks and 13–19 inline style blocks each, which is heavy enough to be worth a proper measurement early. Also outside this audit: Search Console and analytics history, current rankings, backlinks, and the Merchant Centre product feed — all of which need account access rather than a crawl.
